Power Sampling

Power sampling is an inference-time method that sharpens the base model's distribution over whole completions rather than retraining the model.

First Principles

The method upweights high-likelihood full sequences under a powered version of the base distribution and uses iterative MCMC-style resampling to approximate samples from that sharper distribution.

Concrete Example

A base model generates an answer, then resamples blocks of the answer and keeps changes that better fit the sharpened likelihood landscape.

Why It Matters

It suggests that better decoding can recover much of the one-shot reasoning benefit of RL while preserving pass@k diversity.

Common Misunderstanding

Power sampling is not the same as low temperature. Low temperature sharpens local token choices; power sampling tries to account for future completion likelihoods.
