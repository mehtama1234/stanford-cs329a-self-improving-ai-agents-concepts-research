RLHF

RLHF stands for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. After a model learns to predict words by reading text, RLHF teaches it to prefer certain answers over others by showing it which answers humans liked. It is the tuning step that makes a model behave the way people want — answering helpfully instead of just guessing the next word.

First Principles

Start with a model that has learned language by predicting the next word in billions of texts. Now show it pairs of answers — one answer it made, and another answer to the same question. Ask humans: which is better? Those humans mark their votes. Next, train a separate checker (called a reward model) to predict what humans will vote for: does it sound clear? helpful? honest? Then, nudge the main model's dials so it becomes more likely to produce answers the reward checker gives high scores to. The model learns to chase the reward checker's approval.

Concrete Example

A model answers 'What is 2+2?' with 'I do not know.' and also generates 'The answer is 4.' Humans vote: the second is better. A reward checker learns this pattern from thousands of votes. Now during training, every time the model writes 'The answer is 4,' the reward goes up. The model's dials shift to make that sentence more likely. After many examples, it reliably picks useful answers.

Why It Matters

Without RLHF, a model is just guessing the next word based on patterns it saw in training text — it might be rude, evasive, or make up facts because all those patterns existed somewhere in the training data. RLHF steers the model toward human preferences: answering directly, helping the person, staying honest. This made AI chat models actually useful to talk to. However, RLHF has tradeoffs: the reward checker can be fooled, the model might say things that sound right without being true, or it might avoid hard questions and only give safe answers. The model chases what the reward checker sees, not what is actually good.

Common Misunderstanding

RLHF uses human judgment as the signal: people decide which answer is better. This is different from RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verification), which uses a rule or automatic checker to grade answers (e.g., does the math work out?). RLHF says humans voted; RLVR says the answer passed a test. RLHF works for subjective goals (being friendly, clear) but struggles with hidden mistakes. RLVR works only where you can write a checker, but it catches objective mistakes RLHF might miss.
