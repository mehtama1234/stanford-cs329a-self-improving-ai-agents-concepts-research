Test-Time Compute

Test-time compute means spending more effort when the model answers a question—not by changing what the model learned, but by giving it more time to try, retry, or check its work. Think of it like redoing a math problem three times and picking the right answer, instead of writing down your first guess and moving on.

First Principles

When a model answers a question, it always picks from a ranked list of possibilities it learned during training. For hard questions, the best answer may not be at the very top of that list, or the model may need to explore several paths to find it. Test-time compute lets you stay with the same model but change how you use it—generate many attempts, check which ones work, refine rough ideas, test answers against rules. The model's learning does not change; only the way you run it gets smarter.

Concrete Example

You ask an AI to write a program that sorts numbers. Instead of writing one program and hoping it works, you tell it to write 50 different versions. You test each one with sample numbers. You keep the one that gets them right every time. One model, same brain, but by spending time to generate and test many answers, you get a much better result.

Why It Matters

Reasoning models are designed to think step by step, and agents learn by trying and adjusting. Most hard problems—coding, math, planning—are not solved by thinking just once. They need you to explore possibilities, double-check work, catch mistakes, and steer effort to the hardest parts. Test-time compute is how you get a fixed model to solve harder problems by giving it time to do that work at answer-time instead of requiring it to learn it all during training.

Common Misunderstanding

Test-time compute is not giving the model superpowers it did not have before. It only works if you have a way to tell good answers from bad ones—a test that passes, a rule that the answer must follow, or another checker you trust. If you just generate 50 answers and pick randomly, you get nowhere. The extra thinking only buys you better results when you can spot the better result.
