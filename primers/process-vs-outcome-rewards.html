Process Rewards Versus Outcome Rewards

When training AI to reason step-by-step, you can reward it in two ways. An outcome reward gives a score only at the very end — did you get the final answer right or wrong? A process reward gives scores all along the way — was your second step correct? Your third? Both approaches try to make good reasoning more likely, but they look at different moments in the chain.

First Principles

Imagine a student solving a hard math problem on paper. If you only grade the final answer, a lucky guess looks the same as careful work — and careless mistakes in the middle hide until they blow up at the end. If you grade each step, you catch wrong moves immediately and reward the student for sound reasoning even if a small slip ruins the finale. Outcome rewards are easier to check because the final answer is often simple to verify (right or wrong). Process rewards are harder because you must build a judge smart enough to evaluate intermediate work — but if you get that judge right, it can give clearer guidance for what good reasoning looks like.

Concrete Example

Say you're training a model to solve geometry proofs. An outcome reward waits until the proof is finished, then marks it correct or incorrect — one score for the whole thing. A process reward evaluates each logical step: Does step 2 follow from step 1? Is that angle calculation valid? That check, that check, then the final answer check. For training, process rewards let the model learn what correct reasoning looks like in the middle, before a small mistake at the end erase all the good work.

Why It Matters

Process rewards are part of how reasoning models become better at thinking. When a model gets denser feedback — scores for each step, not just the end — it can learn finer patterns in what sound reasoning looks like. This matters because bad reasoning can hide inside a correct answer (maybe the model guessed right despite wrong steps), and good reasoning can be punished by a single error at the end. By grading the journey and not just the destination, you give the model a clearer map of what to do next time.

Common Misunderstanding

Process rewards sound like they always give better guidance, but that is not true. If your step-checker is wrong — if it rewards polished-sounding nonsense or punishes valid shortcuts — it teaches the model bad lessons. A process reward is only as good as the judge you build for it. A sloppy process judge can make training worse than just grading the final answer, because the model learns to chase the wrong target. The hard part is not having a process judge; the hard part is making sure that judge actually knows what it is checking.
