Supervised Finetuning

Supervised finetuning is the phase where you take a model that has already learned language basics and show it hundreds or thousands of specific examples: here is a customer question — here is the perfect answer you should give. After seeing many pairs like this, the model learns to copy that style and reasoning pattern.

First Principles

Before finetuning, a model learns from reading billions of words in any order — just getting good at the next-word guessing game (this is pretraining). Finetuning comes next: instead of random text, you feed it pairs of prompt-and-response you wrote or collected, with a label saying 'this response is right.' The model then nudges its dials to make responses like that more likely. Over time, it stops acting like a generic text-guesser and starts acting like a helper — or a reasoner, or a coder, depending on what pairs you showed it.

Concrete Example

Say you work at a pizza restaurant and want to train a helper chatbot. You collect 500 real customer messages ('Can I order gluten-free pizza?', 'What's your delivery time?') paired with ideal replies your manager wrote ('Yes, we offer gluten-free crust...', 'We deliver in 30-45 minutes...'). You show the model these 500 pairs. After seeing thousands of similar pairs (your team writes more), the bot starts answering new questions in that exact style — factual, friendly, specific to pizza.

Why It Matters

Finetuning is the simplest and cheapest way to teach a model a job. Want it to write essays in short sentences? Give it essays written that way. Want it to show its working on a math problem? Give it math problems with step-by-step solutions, and it learns to do that. Want it to refuse dangerous requests? Give it dangerous requests paired with refusals. You can also use finetuning to copy reasoning traces from a smarter model into a smaller one — a technique called distillation, which uses finetuning as its engine.

Common Misunderstanding

People often think finetuning is just for manners — making a model say 'please' and 'thank you.' That is wrong. Finetuning copies whatever pattern you show it: a step-by-step reasoning process, a code-writing style, a specific language, even a new kind of thinking the base model never learned. If your finetuning pairs contain 'think hard, list your reasoning, then answer,' the model adopts that reasoning habit, not just word choice.
