Power Sampling

Power sampling is a way to get better answers from a language model without retraining it. Instead of asking the model once and trusting the first answer, power sampling asks the model to generate multiple answers and then picks the best ones using a sharper version of the model's own confidence rankings — like turning up the contrast on what the model thinks is good versus bad.

First Principles

A language model always assigns a confidence score to every word or phrase it could say next. Power sampling takes those scores and exaggerates the differences — high-confidence answers get pushed higher, low-confidence ones lower. It then uses a resampling loop (drawing new candidate answers repeatedly, keeping the ones that fit the sharpened scores) to approximate what samples would look like if the model had been trained on that exaggerated distribution. The result: answer blocks that the model already ranked as likely get refined further, without changing the model's weights.

Concrete Example

Imagine a model generates a full answer to a math problem. Power sampling then re-examines chunks of that answer — say, the first paragraph or the calculation step — and asks: under my sharpened confidence scoring, can I find a better version of this chunk that still makes sense? It redraws alternatives and keeps ones that score higher on this sharper scale. After iterating this process, you get an answer that is not just what the model thought first, but what it thinks after using better judgment.

Why It Matters

Retraining a model (via reinforcement learning) to give better step-by-step answers takes enormous compute and can break the model's existing skills. Power sampling gets many of those benefits — cleaner reasoning, higher accuracy — by just using smarter decoding at inference time. It also preserves diversity (you can sample multiple good answers, not just one), which helps catch cases where the model's first guess was not the only right answer.

Common Misunderstanding

Power sampling is often confused with temperature scaling, which lowers the temperature knob on a model to make it more confident. But temperature is a one-step adjustment: it makes each individual word choice more or less random. Power sampling works differently — it looks at full answer blocks and re-judges them under a sharpened scoring system, accounting for how well the entire completion fits, not just whether the next word is likely. Lower temperature makes one word pickier; power sampling makes the model rethink whole sequences.
