Distillation

Distillation trains one model to imitate another model, usually to transfer capability into a cheaper, smaller, or more specialized model.

First Principles

A teacher model produces outputs, reasoning traces, labels, preferences, or demonstrations. A student model trains on those artifacts. The student may learn behavior that would be expensive to discover from raw data alone.

Concrete Example

A strong reasoning model solves many math problems step by step. A smaller model is finetuned on those solutions and becomes much better at similar math tasks.

Why It Matters

Distillation can expand a student's practical reasoning boundary more than RL alone because it supplies explicit high-quality worked solutions rather than only a final right-or-wrong score.

Common Misunderstanding

Distillation is not always about making a smaller clone. A larger student can also improve by learning from generated data from a different teacher.
