GRPO

GRPO is a group-relative RL method that compares multiple answers to the same prompt and rewards answers that are better than the group average.

First Principles

Instead of needing a separate 'critic' model — a helper trained to predict how good an answer will turn out — GRPO samples a group of completions for one problem, scores them, and uses relative performance inside the group as the learning signal.

Concrete Example

For one math problem, the model generates eight answers. Three are correct and five are wrong. The correct ones are reinforced relative to the wrong ones.

Why It Matters

GRPO helped make RLVR practical for reasoning because it reduces infrastructure complexity and uses the natural contrast among sampled attempts.

Common Misunderstanding

GRPO does not learn from a problem if every sampled answer is equally good or equally bad. The useful signal comes from contrast.
