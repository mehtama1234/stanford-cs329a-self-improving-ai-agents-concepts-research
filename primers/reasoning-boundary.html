Reasoning Boundary

The reasoning boundary is the set of problems a model can solve at least sometimes under some sampling or inference procedure.

First Principles

A model's practical ability has two parts: what solutions exist in its distribution and how often those solutions are sampled. RL can increase sampling frequency without necessarily expanding the set of possible solutions.

Concrete Example

If a base model can solve a problem once in 500 attempts, that problem is inside its broad boundary but outside its reliable one-shot behavior.

Why It Matters

This concept separates reliability from capability. It helps interpret papers claiming that RL mostly elicits existing reasoning rather than creating new reasoning.

Common Misunderstanding

The boundary is not identical to normal benchmark accuracy. Benchmark accuracy usually measures only a narrow inference setup.
