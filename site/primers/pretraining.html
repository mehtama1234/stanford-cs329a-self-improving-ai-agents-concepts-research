Pretraining

Pretraining is the broad learning phase where a model predicts the next token across a huge corpus and absorbs language, facts, patterns, styles, and many weak forms of reasoning.

First Principles

The model is not directly taught to be helpful or to solve tasks. It is repeatedly asked: given this context, what token is likely next? To reduce error, it must build internal representations of grammar, concepts, procedures, facts, code, mathematical patterns, and the distribution of human-written reasoning.

Concrete Example

If the text says 'To solve 2x + 3 = 7, subtract 3 from both sides...', the model learns that algebra explanations often continue with a transformation step. Across many examples, this creates latent algebraic behavior.

Why It Matters

Most later alignment, reasoning, RL, and agentic behavior sits on top of pretraining. If the base model does not already contain useful representations, post-training has little to elicit.

Common Misunderstanding

Pretraining is not just memorization. It includes memorization, but the useful part is compression: learning reusable structure that generalizes across contexts.
