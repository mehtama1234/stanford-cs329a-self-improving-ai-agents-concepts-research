Base Model Capability

A base model is often more capable than one ordinary sample suggests because its probability distribution contains many possible reasoning paths, including rare good ones.

First Principles

A language model defines a distribution over completions. One answer is only one draw from that distribution. Capability is therefore not just what appears in the first sample; it also includes what can be found through repeated sampling, search, or better decoding.

Concrete Example

A base model may answer a math problem incorrectly on its first attempt, but among 100 attempts it may produce a fully correct solution. The skill was present, but not reliably selected.

Why It Matters

This explains why pass@k, power sampling, best-of-k, and test-time compute can make base models look much stronger without changing weights.

Common Misunderstanding

A bad first answer does not prove the model lacks the ability. It may prove that the default sampling method failed to elicit the ability.
