Mid-Training

Mid-training is a training phase that sits between two big stages: first, the model reads a mountain of general text and learns to predict the next word well; second, it gets tuned to follow instructions and respond helpfully. Mid-training is the step in between where you feed it a specific diet of data — like lots of code examples, math problems, or documents in different languages — to make it especially strong in those areas before the final tuning happens.

First Principles

In the first huge learning phase, a model soaks up broad internet text and gets good at guessing the next word. That gives it general knowledge and reasoning. Then in mid-training, you deliberately steer it toward specific strengths — by showing it high-quality code repeatedly, or dense math papers, or long complicated documents — so it tunes its internal dials to handle those domains better. Only after that does the final phase begin: teaching it to follow human instructions and rewarding it for helpful, harmless answers.

Concrete Example

Imagine training a student. First, they read many subjects and learn general knowledge. Mid-training is like then saying: 'Now spend months on programming and calculus so you get really sharp at those.' Finally, you teach them how to write clear essays and give good presentations. A large language model follows the same pattern: first broad reading, then focused deep-dives into code and math, then learning to respond as a helpful assistant.

Why It Matters

Modern AI models get dramatically better at certain skills without anyone redesigning how they work — no new parts, no new architecture. Mid-training is a big reason why: you can take a general model and, by feeding it the right specialized data for weeks, make it much stronger at coding, math, reasoning over long texts, or other domains. It is one of the simplest and most powerful levers for improvement, because it works with the model's existing dials and structure.

Common Misunderstanding

Mid-training is not just 'more of the same pretraining.' It is deliberately different. Pretraining means reading random web text to learn language basics. Mid-training means carefully curating your data — selecting code, math, or domain-specific documents — and using a focused training process to shift the model toward those areas. The data mix and the training objective are chosen to move the needle on specific capabilities, not to spread learning thin across everything.
