Latent Reasoning

Latent reasoning means reasoning behavior that the base model can produce but does not reliably expose under ordinary prompting or decoding.

First Principles

Pretraining can install many partial algorithms and reasoning traces. These behaviors may live as low-probability paths. Post-training, prompting, search, or sampling can make them easier to access.

Concrete Example

A base model may rarely generate a correct backtracking strategy. A reasoning-tuned model may use backtracking frequently at the right time.

Why It Matters

It explains why inference-time methods and RL can produce large apparent gains without necessarily adding new knowledge.

Common Misunderstanding

Latent does not mean fake. A low-probability skill can still be a real skill if the model can execute it coherently.
