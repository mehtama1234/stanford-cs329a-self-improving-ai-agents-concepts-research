Sampling

Sampling is the process of choosing tokens from the model's probability distribution during generation.

First Principles

At each step, the model gives probabilities for possible next tokens. Decoding rules decide whether to pick the most likely token, sample randomly, sharpen the distribution, truncate low-probability tokens, or search across multiple possible futures.

Concrete Example

With high temperature, the model may explore more unusual solution paths. With low temperature, it becomes more predictable but may get stuck in a common flawed path.

Why It Matters

Reasoning quality depends heavily on how the model is sampled. Different sampling methods trade off reliability, diversity, cost, and exploration.

Common Misunderstanding

Sampling is not a cosmetic output setting. For reasoning tasks, sampling can determine which internal strategy the model follows.
