reinforcement learning

RL Information Inefficiency

When an AI learns by trial and error (reinforcement learning), it often gets very little useful feedback per attempt, especially on long tasks. A single pass-or-fail grade at the end tells the model almost nothing about where its work went wrong along the way, making learning slow and costly.

First Principles

During pretraining, the model reads text and gets told the right answer on nearly every chunk—like a student who sees the answer key after each sentence. In reinforcement learning, the model might generate thousands of chunks of text before finding out if it succeeded. If success is rare, even a single 'yes' or 'no' carries very little real information about how to do better next time.

Concrete Example

A model spends an hour working through a coding problem—writing functions, testing them, debugging—and at the very end, it learns only 'your solution does not work.' It never learns which line of code was wrong, whether the logic was flawed early on, or what would have been a better approach halfway through.

Why It Matters

Because reward signals are sparse and expensive, humans must design curricula (break problems into easier steps), build checkers that score progress along the way, filter weak solutions out early, and generate new practice problems that match the model's current ability. These techniques aim the learning signals carefully so nothing goes to waste.

Common Misunderstanding

Saying RL is information-inefficient does not mean RL does not work. It means the learning signals are precious and must be shaped carefully. A model can still learn to do hard things via RL—it just requires smart design of what problems to try and what feedback to give.
